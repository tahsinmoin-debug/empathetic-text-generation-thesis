# 3.5 Standards

There is no single engineering standard for an empathetic dialogue system. This project therefore followed the standards, published codes of practice and licence terms that apply to its parts: the data and models it uses, the way it reports results, the professional practice it imitates, and the safety rules that any mental-health-adjacent tool must respect. Table 3.91 lists each one and what was done to comply.

**Data and model licences.** EmpatheticDialogues is used under CC BY-NC 4.0, which permits non-commercial research use {cite:ed_github}; this constrains any later deployment and is stated in Section 3.4.4. Every model used is openly released under a licence that permits research use, and the licence of each is recorded in Table 3.1.

**Practice standards for supportive conversation.** The three response rules are not invented. Acknowledging the feeling corresponds to the emotional-reaction mechanism of the EPITOME framework for text-based support {cite:sharma2020epitome}. Asking exactly one question follows counselling guidance that warns against the question-answer trap, in which repeated questioning turns support into interrogation {cite:samhsa2019tip35}. The length limit follows from the same guidance and from our own measurement that replies within the limit are rated higher by the judges. Section 5.5.8 reports the evidence for each rule, including where our own measures disagree with the rule.

**Evaluation and reporting standards.** Empathy is measured with the classifier from the WASSA 2024 shared task, which is a published, peer-reviewed benchmark for exactly this quantity {cite:giorgi2024wassa, yang2024hyy33}. AI-judge evaluation follows the practice established in the LLM-as-judge literature: blind scoring, an anchored rubric, temperature 0, and a panel drawn from unrelated developers to limit self-preference bias {cite:zheng2023judge, panickssery2024, verga2024}. Statistical reporting follows standard practice for paired comparisons: McNemar's exact test {cite:mcnemar1947}, the Wilcoxon signed-rank test {cite:wilcoxon1945}, bootstrap confidence intervals {cite:efron1993} and Holm correction for multiple comparisons {cite:holm1979}.

**Safety standards.** The World Health Organization's guidance on suicide prevention and the safe handling of people in crisis sets the requirement that this system does not meet and must not be claimed to meet: it has no crisis-detection safeguard and no referral mechanism, so it must not be deployed as a mental-health service in its present form {cite:who2023suicide}.

**Academic and professional conduct.** The work follows BRAC University's academic integrity requirements: all sources are cited, all results come from runs the team performed, errors found during the project are reported rather than removed, and the use of AI assistance is disclosed (Section 3.4.6).
