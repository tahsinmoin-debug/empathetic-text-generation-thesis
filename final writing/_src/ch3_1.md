A system that replies to people describing painful experiences is judged by more than its accuracy. It must work within the hardware a student team owns, respect the licence of the data it learns from, protect what a person types, and never present itself as help it is not qualified to give. This chapter sets out the requirements built to meet those demands, the effect the system could have on people and on the environment, the ethical limits placed on it, the standards it follows, and how the project was planned, its risks handled and its costs contained.

# 3.1 Final Specifications and Requirements

The requirements were fixed before the final round of development and did not change afterwards. Each is written so that it can be checked by measurement rather than opinion; Table 3.90 records how every one was tested and what the test produced.

## 3.1.1 Functional Requirements

- **F1 - Emotion detection from the user's own words:** the system reads the message the user actually writes, not a summary of it, and identifies the emotion among the 32 categories of EmpatheticDialogues {cite:rashkin2019}.
- **F2 - Candidate emotions, not a single guess:** the detector returns several likely emotions with a confidence each, so the generator is not forced to accept one possibly wrong label.
- **F3 - A visible emotional decision:** every reply carries the candidate emotions, the emotion finally used, and the words that drove that decision.
- **F4 - Guided generation:** the generator receives wording prepared for the selected emotion rather than one instruction covering all emotions.
- **F5 - Response rules:** every reply acknowledges the feeling, asks exactly one question and stays within 120 words.
- **F6 - Multi-turn conversation:** the same components hold a conversation over several turns and notice when the emotion changes.

## 3.1.2 Non-Functional Requirements

- **N1 - Hardware budget:** the whole system runs on one consumer GPU with 8 GB of memory {cite:nvidia_rtx4060}.
- **N2 - Privacy by locality:** no user message has to leave the device during normal use.
- **N3 - Response time:** a reply arrives within a few seconds on that hardware.
- **N4 - Reproducibility:** every reported number can be regenerated from saved outputs and scripts, with fixed random seeds.
- **N5 - Inspectability:** each component can be replaced and tested alone, so a failure is traceable to one of them.
- **N6 - Cost:** only openly available models, datasets and free service tiers are used.

## 3.1.3 Constraints and Assumptions

The work was bound by one GPU-equipped machine with 8 GB of video memory, the non-commercial licence of EmpatheticDialogues {cite:ed_github}, the daily limits of free evaluation services, and a fixed academic calendar. Three assumptions are stated openly: that the single emotion label given with each conversation is an acceptable if imperfect reference; that an empathy classifier and AI judges stand in reasonably for human perception, which the human study tests; and that the first message carries enough signal for the detector.
