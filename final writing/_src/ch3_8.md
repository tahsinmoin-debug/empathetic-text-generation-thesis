# 3.8 Economic Analysis

The project was designed to cost as close to nothing as possible, because the constraint a student team faces is the one a small clinic also faces. Every model is openly released, every library open source, the dataset free for non-commercial research, the judges free-tier, and the human study runs on a free questionnaire service. ## 3.8.1 Cost-Benefit of the Main Design Choices

- **Where the cheaper option also won:** RoBERTa-large is 2.9 points more accurate than RoBERTa-base but carries 2.8 times the parameters and 7.4 times the training time, and changed no measure of reply quality when substituted into the final system. Fine-tuning the generator with LoRA cost 43 minutes of GPU time and satisfied all three rules in only 22.3% of replies, because it learns the casual style of the dataset's listeners, while prompting an instruction-tuned model needs no training and reaches 60.0%.
- **Generator size:** here the trade-off runs the other way. A single Qwen2.5-7B model beats our pipeline on rule adherence, empathy-classifier score and judge-rated empathy, so on quality alone it would be the better choice; but on the same GPU our system needs 38% less peak memory, 3.40 GB against 5.47 GB, with no significant difference in response time, and reaches 97.5% of that model's overall judge score.
- **Evaluation method:** human annotation in comparable research is paid work, roughly USD 6 per hour in one published study, whereas the free-tier AI judges produced several hundred ratings each at no cost in exchange for rate limits. The human study checks those judges rather than replacing them.
