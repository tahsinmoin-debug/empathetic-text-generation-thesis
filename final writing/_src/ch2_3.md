# 2.3 Summary of Key Findings

Research on empathetic response generation has moved from small models trained from scratch to large pre-trained language models. Early joint models such as MoEL, MIME and EmpDG treat emotion recognition as an auxiliary task and identify the speaker's emotion in only 30.6-31.2% of cases {cite:lin2019moel, majumder2020mime, li2020empdg, qian2023llm}. Knowledge-enhanced successors, including KEMP, CEM, CASE and ESCM, raise this to 36.6-41.2% by adding commonsense and emotional knowledge {cite:li2022kemp, sabour2022cem, zhou2023case, yang2023escm}, and personality-aware multimodal generation reaches 45.4% on text-only EmpatheticDialogues {cite:wu2025traits}. Dedicated classifiers do better still: a fine-tuned RoBERTa model reaches 61.5% on the first utterance of each conversation {cite:paranjape2020chirpy}, and fine-tuned encoders continue to outperform prompted LLMs at emotion classification {cite:belay2025}. On the generation side, instruction-following LLMs have changed the landscape. Human evaluators rate ChatGPT at 4.64 out of 5 for empathy against about 3.0 for the best trained models {cite:qian2023llm}, and recent systems fine-tune LLMs of 7 to 72 billion parameters with knowledge tools or emotional-support data to improve further {cite:cao2025tooled, wang2025esc}.

These advances leave four problems open. First, **LLM empathy is fluent but difficult to control**. LLMs repeat empathic phrases, forget initial instructions and produce lengthy responses that may be mistaken for greater empathy {cite:sorin2024jmir}, yet none of the EmpatheticDialogues studies reviewed measures whether replies follow explicit interaction rules. Second, **the strongest results depend on resources beyond a consumer GPU**, such as fine-tuning models of tens of billions of parameters, multimodal input, or commercial APIs to which a user's disclosure must be sent. Third, **emotion recognition is often evaluated on an easier input than a deployed system receives**. Classifiers trained on situation descriptions read an explicit summary rather than the user's message {cite:rashkin2019}, and joint models do not expose the emotion they recognise for inspection. Fourth, **the statistical evidence is often thin**. Most studies report differences in automatic metrics or small human studies without paired significance testing, and at least one reports empathy gains that are not statistically significant {cite:rasool2025}. Future work should therefore pursue controllable and inspectable behaviour under realistic resource limits, emotion recognition evaluated on real conversational input, and conclusions supported by appropriate statistical tests.
