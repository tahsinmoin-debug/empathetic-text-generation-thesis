# 2.3 Summary of Key Findings

Seven findings emerge from the work reviewed above. Each one is stated with the evidence behind it and with what this thesis does about it, because together they define both the space this work occupies and the shape of the system built in Chapter 4.

**Pre-training matters more than architecture on this task:** The joint models improve from 30.6% to 41.2% emotion accuracy over four years by adding mixtures of listeners, emotion mimicry, adversarial training, commonsense knowledge, graph alignment and dynamic emotion vectors {cite:lin2019moel, majumder2020mime, li2020empdg, li2022kemp, sabour2022cem, zhou2023case, yang2023escm}, while RoBERTa-GPT2 reaches 51.5% on the same task simply by starting from pre-trained components rather than training from scratch {cite:liu2021robertagpt2}. Ten points separate the two approaches, and the cheaper one wins. The lesson we take is to spend the parameter budget on strong pre-trained models and on how they are connected, rather than on architectural machinery trained on a corpus of 25,000 conversations.

**Large language models write better replies than purpose-built empathetic models, but do not recognise emotion more reliably:** Human raters prefer ChatGPT to CEM by 4.64 against 3.08 out of 5 {cite:qian2023llm}, and a systematic review finds large models rated as empathetic as clinicians or more so {cite:sorin2024jmir}. Yet evaluations of recognition find these models no better than trained classifiers on conversational affect {cite:feng2023affect}, beaten by fine-tuned encoders on multi-label emotion {cite:belay2025}, and challenged by emotions that are implied rather than stated {cite:koga2023implicit}. This asymmetry, strong generation alongside unreliable recognition, is the direct argument for giving recognition to a separate model and letting the generator do only what it is good at.

**A small fine-tuned model can beat a large prompted one at classification:** This is the sharpest piece of evidence for our architecture, and it comes from outside the empathy literature. Belay et al. compared encoder-only, encoder-decoder and decoder-only models on the same multi-label emotion data and concluded that fine-tuning smaller, more efficient pre-trained models still outperforms the zero-shot performance of large language models {cite:belay2025}. A deployed system supplies the matching figure on our own corpus: a RoBERTa classifier over the 32 EmpatheticDialogues emotions reaching about 61.5% {cite:paranjape2020chirpy}. A detector of a few hundred million parameters is therefore not a compromise forced by hardware; on this task it is the better instrument.

**Machine empathy repeats itself, and almost nobody measures it:** The characteristic weakness identified in systematic review is not incompetence but sameness: replies that are fluent, kind, and drawn from a small stock of phrases {cite:sorin2024jmir}. None of the joint models reviewed above report a repetition measure, and reference-based comparison cannot detect the problem, since a repetitive reply can resemble a reference reply closely. This is why a repetition measure is included among our own signals, and why it is reported for every system rather than only for ours.

**Structure helps, and there is more than one way to supply it, each with a price:** Telling the model the emotion, or asking it to reason before answering {cite:qian2023llm, li2024emotioncot}, giving it a knowledge base it can call as a tool {cite:cao2025tooled}, conditioning on inferred personality and visual input {cite:wu2025traits}, fine-tuning with a low-rank adapter {cite:wang2025esc}, or retrieving related passages at generation time {cite:lewis2020rag, rasool2025}: all of these add structure to an otherwise unconstrained generator, and all report gains. What none of them establishes is which is worth its cost under a hardware constraint, and the costs are substantial: 48 GB of GPU memory for tool calling {cite:cao2025tooled}, models up to 72B parameters for the fine-tuned approach {cite:wang2025esc}, and multimodal input with personality labels for the trait-aware approach {cite:wu2025traits}. Chapter 5 carries out that comparison inside 8 GB, including the retrieval variant, which we measure and reject.

**Evaluation is the field's weakest point, and workable alternatives now exist:** Published comparisons rest largely on overlap with a single reference reply, which cannot tell whether a reply acknowledged the feeling or asked five questions in a row, and one recent study reports improvements whose significance tests do not reach the conventional threshold and which no human rated {cite:rasool2025}. The alternatives now available are a classifier trained directly on human empathy ratings {cite:giorgi2024wassa, yang2024hyy33} and panels of language-model judges drawn from different developers, which reduce the self-preference that a single judge shows {cite:zheng2023judge, verga2024, panickssery2024}. These are what allow this thesis to be evaluated on what its replies *do* rather than on what they resemble, and to test every difference statistically.

**Separating the small model from the large one has been proposed, but for a different purpose:** The Hybrid Empathetic Framework pairs a small empathetic model with a large language model and predicts emotion in two stages {cite:yang2024hef}. Our work shares that structure and differs in its purpose: a complete system inside 8 GB of consumer memory, an emotional decision exposed as an auditable record rather than kept as an internal step, and an evaluation built around rule adherence, emotional fidelity, a combined empathy panel and paired statistics.

**The gap this thesis addresses:** No existing work, to our knowledge, builds an empathetic response system that (i) runs entirely within consumer hardware, so that a user's disclosure never leaves the machine, (ii) exposes every part of its emotional decision, from the candidate emotions and their confidence to the words that produced them, for inspection before a reply is generated, and (iii) is evaluated against single-model baselines of both comparable and larger size, on measures that correspond to what a supportive reply must do, with every difference tested statistically. Chapters 4 and 5 build and evaluate exactly that system.
