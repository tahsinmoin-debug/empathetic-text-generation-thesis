# 2.3 Summary of Key Findings

Research on empathetic response generation has moved from small models trained from scratch to large pre-trained language models. Early joint models such as MoEL, MIME and EmpDG treat emotion recognition as an auxiliary task and identify the speaker's emotion in only 30.6-31.2% of cases [20], [21], [22], [23]. Knowledge-enhanced successors, including KEMP, CEM, CASE and ESCM, raise this to 36.6-41.2% by adding commonsense and emotional knowledge [24], [2], [25], [26], and personality-aware multimodal generation reaches 45.4% on text-only EmpatheticDialogues [28]. Dedicated classifiers do better still: a fine-tuned RoBERTa model reaches 61.5% on the first utterance of each conversation [32], and fine-tuned encoders continue to outperform prompted LLMs at emotion classification [33]. On the generation side, instruction-following LLMs have changed the landscape. Human evaluators rate ChatGPT at 4.64 out of 5 for empathy against about 3.0 for the best trained models [23], and recent systems fine-tune LLMs of 7 to 72 billion parameters with knowledge tools or emotional-support data to improve further [27], [30].

These advances leave four problems open. First, **LLM empathy is fluent but difficult to control**. LLMs repeat empathic phrases, forget initial instructions and produce lengthy responses that may be mistaken for greater empathy [29], yet none of the EmpatheticDialogues studies reviewed measures whether replies follow explicit interaction rules. Second, **the strongest results depend on resources beyond a consumer GPU**, such as fine-tuning models of tens of billions of parameters, multimodal input, or commercial APIs to which a user's disclosure must be sent. Third, **emotion recognition is often evaluated on an easier input than a deployed system receives**. Classifiers trained on situation descriptions read an explicit summary rather than the user's message [1], and joint models do not expose the emotion they recognise for inspection. Fourth, **the statistical evidence is often thin**. Most studies report differences in automatic metrics or small human studies without paired significance testing, and at least one reports empathy gains that are not statistically significant [31]. Future work should therefore pursue controllable and inspectable behaviour under realistic resource limits, emotion recognition evaluated on real conversational input, and conclusions supported by appropriate statistical tests.

This thesis addresses these gaps as summarised in Table 2.2.

|---|---|---|---|
